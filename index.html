The Four Functions of Money Explained

Discover how money makes our economy work through real-life examples from a typical high school student's day.

What is Money?

Money is far more than just coins and paper in your wallet. It serves four critical functions that make our economy work smoothly every single day.

Today, we'll follow Alex, a high school student, through an ordinary day and discover how money performs all four functions.

Key Questions to Consider:

  • Why do we use money instead of bartering?
  • How does money help us plan for the future?
  • Why can we buy things we can't afford right now?

🔄 Function 1: Medium of Exchange

Money is used to buy and sell goods and services. It is the most common method of payment for transactions.

Why It Matters

Without money, society would operate on a barter system — directly trading goods for other goods. This is inefficient because:

  • You need someone who has what you want AND wants what you have
  • It's difficult to divide goods fairly
  • Some items can't be easily traded

Real-Life Example: Alex Buys Lunch

12:15 PM - School Cafeteria

Alex is hungry and needs food. He pays $12 with his debit card and gets a chicken wrap, chips, and a drink.

Without Money (Barter): Alex would need to trade something valuable for food — but the cafeteria worker probably doesn't want his textbook!

With Money: Transaction complete in 30 seconds. Both parties are happy!

Why This Function Matters

✅ Alex gets food he wants without complicated negotiations
✅ The cafeteria worker gets accepted payment they can use elsewhere
✅ Both parties are satisfied with a simple transaction

🏦 Function 2: Store of Value

Money can be saved and stored without losing its worth, allowing people to preserve purchasing power for future use.

Why It Matters

A store of value lets you:

  • Save for goals: College tuition, car, gaming console
  • Preserve wealth: Instead of spending everything immediately
  • Plan ahead: Have money available for emergencies
  • Build financial security: Create a safety net

📊 Function 3: Unit of Account

Money provides a common measure of value that allows us to compare the worth of different goods and services.

Why It Matters

Without a unit of account, comparing things is hard:

  • Is a video game worth more than shoes?
  • How many lunches equal one backpack?
  • What's a fair price for a haircut?

Money, measured in dollars and cents, gives us a standard way to compare value!

Real-Life Example: Alex Spends His Weekly Allowance

Budget: $60 to spend this week

Option 1: Video Game
Cost: $60 | Value: 60 hours of entertainment over 2 months

Option 2: School Lunches
Cost: $12 per lunch | Can buy: 5 lunches | Value: Fuel for the week

Option 3: Movie Tickets
Cost: $15 per ticket | Can buy: 4 tickets | Value: 8 hours with friends

Option 4: New Clothing

The Insight

By expressing everything in dollars, Alex can easily compare and decide what gives him the most value:

1 video game ($60) = 5 lunches ($60) = 4 movie tickets ($60) = 1 new shirt ($60)

⏰ Function 4: Standard of Deferred Payment

Money allows people to make agreements about payments in the future. It enables credit, loans, and the ability to pay for things over time.

Why It Matters

Without this function, you could only buy things you can afford RIGHT NOW. This would mean:

  • No college education (too expensive upfront)
  • No houses
  • No emergency medical care without cash
  • No business growth
